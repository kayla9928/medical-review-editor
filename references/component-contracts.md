# Component Contracts

Use this reference for independent submission components. For journal
adaptation or a hard format limit, establish the applicable fields, limits, and
labels; verified requirements override these functional defaults. Ordinary
content drafting may use supplied requirements or the defaults below without
automatically initiating journal research or an extra confirmation step.

| Component | Reader and function | Must carry | Must not carry / division of labour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Abstract | Editors, indexers, and time-limited clinical readers; enables accurate triage | Review focus, scope, evidence approach when required, central synthesis, and decision-relevant boundaries | Do not become a compressed full text, a reference list, or a Conclusion substitute. Its similarly named fields need not perform the same function as body sections. |
| Article Highlights | Rapid editorial and reader scan | A small set of distinct, clinically meaningful takeaways aligned with the review | Do not repeat the abstract sentence by sentence, introduce unverified claims, or serve as a methods/results section. |
| Keywords | Indexing and retrieval | Standard, specific terms for condition, population, intervention or concept as applicable | Do not make claims, restate the title mechanically, or replace controlled vocabulary where required. |
| Important-reference annotations | Editors and readers assessing selected source relevance | Why the already selected source is pivotal and what claim, method, or turning point it supports | Do not reopen literature selection, rank the field comprehensively, or introduce unsupported conclusions. |
| Running title / short title | Readers and editorial or production systems identifying the article in a compact, stand-alone field | Enough topic identity to distinguish the article and, when space permits, its most important organizing perspective, population, or clinical orientation | Do not become a mini-abstract, report a result, mechanically truncate the full title, or meet a limit through silent scope drift, opaque abbreviation, or concept substitution. |
| Title and other stand-alone units | Editors and readers identifying scope and promise | Accurate topic, population/setting or review type when material, and the manuscript's real organizing focus | Do not prove claims, add unverified novelty, duplicate abstract functions, or conceal material scope limits. |

Before designing or drafting, establish the authorized component(s), applicable
format constraints, permitted manuscript/source scope, protected author intent
and wording, verification boundary, and relevant adjacent components. Resolve
only missing information that materially changes the requested output. Keep
two distinct products:

- `component-design`: return a field/function and information-allocation plan,
  not manuscript-ready prose;
- `component-draft`: convert authorized inputs into a complete candidate unit.

Design and drafting are output choices, not mandatory approval stages. If
drafting is authorized and inputs suffice, deliver it. Every English component
uses `language-and-terminology.md`.

For an authorized contribution-alignment task, reuse the cross-section
understanding described in `structure-and-prose.md`. The Introduction builds
the problem and purpose, the Conclusion synthesizes what the review supports,
and the Abstract independently identifies its focus, synthesis, and clinical
meaning. Share the argument, not identical sentences or section duties.

## Abstract

Follow journal-specified structured fields when present. For an unstructured
narrative-review abstract with no user-specified organization, use this default
sequence:

1. state the clinical problem or unresolved limitation without generic
   background;
2. introduce the organizing perspective or genuine change in understanding,
   without manufacturing a paradigm shift;
3. define the review scope and synthesis using verbs that match what the review
   actually does;
4. state the clinical implication and any boundary that changes interpretation
   or use.

Treat 150 to 250 words and mostly 15 to 25 words per sentence only as soft
defaults when no journal limit exists. Choose tense by information function,
not by a mechanical template. Apply the routed terminology and independent
abbreviation-domain rules, and do not add claims absent from the authorized
manuscript or evidence.

## Manuscript Title

Use a concise noun phrase by default, while preserving the actual scope,
population or setting when material, and organizing perspective. Remove words
that add no scope or relationship, but retain necessary prepositions. Avoid
promotional novelty or benefit claims. When quantity is unspecified, provide
one preferred title and two alternatives with materially different scope,
organizing perspective, or clinical emphasis; do not use marketing labels for
the alternatives.

## Running Titles And Other Hard-Limit Short Fields

Treat a running title as a compact article identifier, not as a mechanically
truncated full title. Before drafting, record the exact field label, counting
unit and limit, whether spaces and punctuation count, supplied capitalization
or abbreviation rules, protected medical scope and terminology, and any form
the author has explicitly excluded. Do not present an inferred convention as a
journal requirement.

Test feasibility before optimizing wording. If the full topic identity,
organizing perspective, clinical orientation, protected terms, and format
preferences cannot all fit, state the incompatible combination and identify
which information layer each candidate compresses or omits. Do not conceal the
conflict by:

- substituting a shorter near-synonym for a protected disciplinary concept;
- broadening or narrowing the population, disease, intervention, or setting
  without making that tradeoff visible;
- inventing an abbreviation, compressed compound, or punctuation device;
- deleting normal spaces or truncating a word; or
- presenting an author-created framework as a validated category.

Build an information budget with four classes: must retain, may compress
without semantic change, may omit for this field, and must not add. Candidate
quantity follows the journal contract, the author's request, or the confirmed
component design. When multiple candidates are requested, make them differ in
meaningful information allocation, such as topic specificity, organizing
perspective, or clinical orientation, rather than by superficial synonym
replacement.

Mechanically validate each exact final string using its final spaces,
punctuation, hyphens, and capitalization. Report the count when it is material
to selection. An author rejection of a structure or punctuation form becomes
a constraint for the current component iteration, but it does not become a
universal rule for other authors, components, or projects.

Keep status explicit: generated forms remain candidates until the author
selects one. Do not reopen an author-selected or confirmed running title unless
revision or audit is separately authorized.

## Article Highlights

Give each highlight one independent, clinically meaningful job. Do not repeat
the abstract sentence by sentence or introduce new evidence. Use sentences or
fragments according to the journal contract, not a universal template.

## Keywords

Verify the journal's number and controlled-vocabulary requirements. When none
are specified, use five to seven relevant terms. Prefer verified MeSH or other
required controlled terms, but do not claim a term is controlled without
checking. Do not add traffic-oriented terms that misrepresent the manuscript,
mechanically ban all hierarchical overlap, or broaden the review's scope.

## Important-Reference Annotations

Use parallel syntax and a consistent basis for explaining why each already
selected source matters. Reserve labels such as `landmark` or `seminal` for
cases with support for that status. Terminology or sentence normalization does
not authorize reselection, reranking, or addition of references.
