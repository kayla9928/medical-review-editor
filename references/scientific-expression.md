# Scientific Expression And Lexical Precision

Use this reference for concision and lexical-choice decisions in medical and
SCI text. Treat every cue as context-dependent unless it is marked as a hard
rule.

## H: Hard Rules

1. Generate no em dash in new or revised manuscript text. Recast the relation
   with clauses, commas, semicolons, colons, or separate sentences. Preserve
   excluded quotations and official names.
2. Never apply an avoid/use-instead list mechanically. Compare meaning, actor,
   action, causality, quantity, strength, terminology, genre, and function.
3. Never simplify when the shorter form changes meaning, population,
   condition, actor, quantity, citation attachment, or author stance.

## M1: High-Priority Concision Cues

Consider compression only when the proposition and local function remain
identical:

- causal or conditional shells that repeat an already explicit relation;
- time or process phrases that can be stated directly without losing sequence;
- quantity or degree wording that repeats the same magnitude;
- duplicated components with the same local job;
- nominalizations that obscure the responsible actor or action;
- empty metadiscourse that contributes no section function; and
- indirect action phrases whose direct verb preserves agency and strength.

Recheck citation attachment and sentence logic after every compression.

## M2: Semantic And Scientific-Action Alerts

Pause rather than auto-replace when a candidate changes a disciplinary concept,
measurement operation, evidence posture, recommendation identity, or count.
Common alerts include:

- `methodology` versus `method`;
- `parameter` versus `limit`;
- `capability` versus `ability`;
- `caveat` versus `warning`;
- `causal factor` versus `cause`;
- `majority` versus `most`;
- `detect` versus `measure`;
- `apparent` versus `clear`;
- `it is believed` versus `I think`;
- `it is recommended` versus `we recommend`;
- `would seem to indicate` versus `indicates`;
- singular `cell` versus plural `cells`;
- singular or plural treatment of `data` under the target convention; and
- `well known` versus `established`.

Use the form supported by the authorized evidence, genre, actor, and confirmed
author decision. A shorter expression is not automatically more accurate.

## T: Article-Type And Section Boundaries

Match actions to the article type and section. A review usually `synthesizes`,
`examines`, `compares`, or `organizes` published evidence; original research may
`recruit`, `measure`, `estimate`, `model`, or `observe` its own data. Methods,
Results, statistical reporting, qualitative results, and case reports require
their own genre invariants. Do not import review-language actions into those
units or treat a review's author synthesis as a primary empirical result.

## X: Resistance Tests

Reject a proposed simplification if any answer is yes:

1. Does it change what was done, observed, inferred, or recommended?
2. Does it change a technical category, quantity, grammatical number, or actor?
3. Does it strengthen association into causation or suggestion into proof?
4. Does it turn passive institutional guidance into an author recommendation,
   or the reverse?
5. Does it move a statement from review synthesis toward original-research
   reporting?
6. Does it detach a citation or collapse functionally distinct repetition?

When uncertain, preserve the current wording or present the issue for author or
domain confirmation.
