# Visual And Table Alignment

## Principle

Figures, tables, legends, and prose should complement each other. The prose
should not mechanically repeat every figure node or table cell.

## Titles, Headers, And Cells

- Use concise noun phrases for figure and table titles, identifying the object,
  comparison, or clinical setting. Remove generic format labels only when they
  add no identifying information.
- Keep peer headers grammatically parallel and make each column or row compare
  the same kind of information.
- Use compact entries in cells or figure nodes, but do not remove conditions,
  hierarchy, uncertainty, timing, or exception status needed for correct use.
- Move necessary explanatory prose out of crowded cells or nodes and into the
  proper table footnote or figure legend.

## Table Footnotes And Figure Legends

Use table footnotes to define symbols, conditions, exceptions, recommendation
levels, and necessary methodological details. Keep each superscript marker
uniquely attached to its target. Do not use footnotes as a second Discussion.

Use figure legends to identify visible objects, symbols, directions, colors,
branches, and conditions required for stand-alone interpretation. Do not repeat
every node or add a medical interpretation unsupported by the figure, text, or
evidence. Keep figure legends and table footnotes as distinct components.

Define every abbreviation actually used in the stand-alone figure or table.
Follow journal ordering and punctuation; when unspecified, use one consistent
alphabetical order. Do not mechanically duplicate full-form definitions inside
cells when the legend or footnote already supplies an unambiguous definition.

## Figure/Algorithm Audit

Check:

- entry conditions and target population;
- branch labels and timing;
- treatment identity and hierarchy;
- optional, alternative, rescue, adjunctive, and investigational status;
- endpoints, reassessment, and escalation;
- abbreviation definitions;
- legend consistency with visible content;
- whether visual claims exceed text or evidence support.

## Table Audit

Check:

- whether rows compare the same type of object;
- source identity and recommendation strength;
- population, clinical scenario, and condition attachment;
- slash, plus, optional adjunct, and sequence meanings;
- whether notes preserve hierarchy and uncertainty;
- whether prose and table make the same claim at different levels of detail.

## Output

When auditing visuals or tables, report each issue with:

- visual/table location;
- source anchor or visible element;
- problem;
- risk;
- revision direction;
- whether evidence verification is needed.
