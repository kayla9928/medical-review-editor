# Structure And Prose

## Section Function

Before revising, identify the section's job:

- Introduction: establish problem, gap, and review purpose.
- Mechanism/background: explain why the phenomenon matters.
- Classification/framework: define categories, boundaries, and clinical use.
- Guideline/evidence synthesis: state what sources support and where they
  disagree.
- Treatment/management: explain target selection, sequencing, escalation, and
  limits.
- Future directions: define unresolved evidence and implementation needs.

Do not let every section become a guideline summary, mechanism chapter, or
algorithm narrative.

## Revision Priorities

1. Keep one paragraph, one job.
2. Lead with the point, not with a caveat.
3. Put definitions before applying them.
4. Compress recurrence only when both the proposition and its local function
   repeat; preserve functionally distinct recurrence.
5. Preserve useful authorial claims when support is adequate.

## Argument And Paragraph Design

Separate what the passage must establish from how its paragraphs establish it.
Check whether proposed parallel items are independent considerations, causes
and mediators, conditions, or goals and means. Preserve the author's intended
relationship; do not flatten a causal chain into a list.

For the authorized unit, identify:

- the reader's intended conclusion and the paragraph's local job;
- premises that need development, premises established earlier that need only
  brief inheritance, and material reserved for another unit;
- the author-approved functional order and necessary level of detail.

An author's explanation of a mechanism or correction of the editor's reasoning
does not itself authorize retelling that mechanism in the current paragraph.
Likewise, repeating a necessary datum or anatomical feature can support a new
inference; do not remove it merely because it occurred earlier. Use enough
specificity to make the inference intelligible without reopening excluded
evidence or importing new facts.

Preserve an effective author sequence, such as features leading to an
implication followed by treatment objectives. Do not enforce one universal
paragraph pattern. For direct synthesis from multiple materials, organize by
the approved argument rather than by source order. Critical synthesis need not
be a sequence of source summaries or reflexive criticisms.

## Editing Constraints And Prose Content

Treat instructions such as 'do not repeat the mechanism', 'do not add a new
classification', or 'do not expand surgery' as selection constraints. Apply
them through omission, scope, and organization; do not automatically write
their negations into the manuscript. Constraints are not mandatory topics.

When a boundary is needed for correct reader interpretation, retain it as a
scoped scientific statement. `claim-calibration.md` governs its evidentiary
strength and expression. Do not suppress a necessary limitation to avoid
defensive language.

## Contribution And Cross-Section Alignment

Only when the authorized task concerns the review's overall gap, purpose, or
contribution, inspect the roles of all major sections in the available permitted
context. Distinguish mechanistic explanation, guideline/evidence synthesis, and
clinical application. A recent local revision does not define the whole
review's contribution. Do not claim coverage when relevant context is absent;
ask or state the exact limitation only if it changes the design.

Use this shared understanding to align Introduction, Conclusion, and Abstract
without giving them identical jobs or wording. It does not require changing
chapter order or rereading the whole paper for an ordinary local edit.

For linked revisions, identify the approved change and each unit's actual
dependency on it. Separate necessary synchronization, optional improvement,
and unaffected text. Revise only the smallest authorized dependent surface;
related terminology is not sufficient reason to rewrite a paragraph.

## Transition To Drafting

Hand the selected content, relationships, local function, protected order,
evidence limits, and constraints to language realization. The design is not
manuscript wording. Apply `language-and-terminology.md` even when the user has
approved the logic. End the unit with its specific implication or necessary
bridge, without automatically repeating its premises.

## Section Headings

- Use concise noun phrases by default and keep syntax parallel within the same
  hierarchy level.
- Treat five to eight words as a soft target, not a limit that overrides
  accuracy.
- Use a colon pattern only when peer headings express the same relationship.
- Do not mix noun phrases, gerunds, and complete sentences at one level without
  a functional reason.
- Use established abbreviations under the routed terminology rules. This
  section governs manuscript headings; the manuscript title belongs to the
  component contract.

## Common Fixes

- Convert throat-clearing openings into direct topic sentences.
- Move necessary limitations to the sentence or paragraph where they change
  interpretation.
- For clinical-decision paragraphs, consider `clinical scenario -> therapeutic
  target -> modality selection -> escalation` when it fits the actual reasoning.
  Do not impose it on mechanisms, classifications, evidence syntheses, or other
  section functions.
- Avoid section-to-section cross-references unless journal style or reader
  navigation requires them.
