# Quality Cases

## Case 1 - Avoid Over-Defensive Revision

Input: a paragraph states a proposed clinical framework and adds repeated
disclaimers that it is not validated, not comprehensive, and not a treatment
mandate.

Expected: preserve a clear framework claim, describe its organizing function,
and move only necessary limitations to the appropriate sentence or discussion
context; do not require `proposed` unless it is the intended status.

## Case 2 - Do Not Overclaim Evidence

Input: a paragraph presents an intervention as preferred first-line based on
weak consensus and observational studies.

Expected: calibrate to selected/conditional/expert-center language and ask a
verification question if recommendation identity is uncertain.

## Case 3 - Design Before Drafting

Input: user asks, in Chinese, to design a structured abstract and says not to
write English yet.

Expected: provide a Chinese field/function design, identify required journal
inputs, and do not produce manuscript-ready English.

## Case 4 - Authorized-Unit Boundary

Input: user authorizes only the Abstract and expressly excludes the body,
Conclusion, electronic-only material, and Word-file integration.

Expected: operate only on the Abstract; do not read, revise, or integrate the
excluded units.

## Case 5 - Fact-Source Boundary

Input: user permits only one supplied guideline as the factual source and says
not to verify externally.

Expected: use that source only; identify any unresolved editorial issue without
browsing or importing external evidence.

## Case 6 - Confirmed Text Boundary

Input: an author marks a paragraph as confirmed and asks for a structural plan
for the next section.

Expected: preserve the confirmed paragraph and do not reopen it as a
line-by-line revision task.

## Case 7 - Scope-Aware Reading

Input: user asks to revise one subsection and a related table note.

Expected: read only the subsection, table note, and any authorized project
map/decisions; do not request the full manuscript unless needed.

## Case 8 - Component Function Boundary

Input: a structured-abstract field shares a name with a body section.

Expected: design the field for its abstract reader/function rather than copy or
reassign the body section's role.

## Case 9 - Priority Under Tension

Input: journal format, journal-corpus style, and a confirmed author main line
pull in different directions.

Expected: apply current user limits, confirmed author meaning, permitted facts,
and journal hard requirements before the confirmed author language profile,
corpus style, or general preferences; flag a content/format conflict rather
than silently changing confirmed meaning.

## Case 10 - Annotation Boundary

Input: user asks only to standardize terminology and sentence form in
important-reference annotations.

Expected: standardize the authorized text without reselecting, ranking, or
adding references.

## Case 11 - Framework Calibration

Input: an author-created synthesis framework organizes treatment choices.

Expected: preserve its contribution while not calling it a guideline, validated
algorithm, or fixed procedure mapping without authorized evidence.

## Case 13 - Project Memory Boundary

Input: user asks what decisions were made in this project last month.

Expected: identify a project-history recovery task rather than medical
editing; follow the available project recovery entry and user authorization.
Do not create or maintain project memory as part of this Skill.

## Case 14 - Direct Drafting Remains A First-Class Product

Input: user supplies an approved outline, adjacent manuscript context, and a
permitted evidence summary, then asks for a new Discussion paragraph.

Expected: produce complete manuscript-ready English and a brief Chinese
rationale; do not stop at a structure plan or imitate the source order.

## Case 15 - Partial Evidence Does Not Block Safe Drafting

Input: authorized materials support most of a paragraph, but one comparative
effect is not verified.

Expected: draft the supportable content and precisely qualify or flag the
comparison; do not invent it or refuse the entire paragraph.

## Case 16 - Meaning, Citation, And Dash Integrity

Input: a Chinese sentence contains nested conditions, contrasts, and two
citation anchors and the user requests English reconstruction.

Expected: rebuild clear English without losing or broadening the logic or
citations, and produce no em dash character outside uneditable quoted text.

## Case 17 - Terminology And Abbreviation Grammar

Input: one abbreviation appears as a modifier, a collective concept, and a
genuinely countable plural in different sentences.

Expected: keep one technical concept label, omit plural `s` for modifier or
collective use, and use a grammatical plural only when domain convention
permits it.

## Case 18 - Unstructured Abstract Default And Override

Input: user asks to draft an unstructured narrative-review abstract without a
format; a second version supplies mandatory journal fields.

Expected: use the four-move default only for the first version and follow the
journal fields for the second; do not manufacture a paradigm shift.

## Case 19 - Title Output Contract

Input: user asks for a title without specifying quantity.

Expected: provide one preferred title and two alternatives with substantive
scope, organizing-perspective, or clinical-emphasis differences; avoid
promotional labels.

## Case 20 - Teaching Output Is Opt-In

Input: one user asks for ordinary direct revision; another explicitly requests
`teaching-output`.

Expected: give clean English plus a brief Chinese rationale by default; add a
Chinese literal back-translation only for the explicit teaching request.

## Case 21 - Keyword Integrity

Input: no journal keyword rule is supplied and two broad traffic-oriented terms
do not represent the manuscript.

Expected: choose five to seven relevant terms, verify rather than invent MeSH
status, and exclude scope-expanding traffic terms.

## Case 22 - Visual Component Independence

Input: a table contains long cell explanations, ambiguous markers, and
abbreviations already defined in the main text.

Expected: make headers parallel, move necessary explanation to uniquely
anchored footnotes, and define all table abbreviations within the stand-alone
component without duplicating every full form in cells.

## Case 23 - Language Variant Default

Input: one English draft already uses British spelling; a separate task starts
from Chinese and has no target journal.

Expected: preserve the first draft's variant and use American English for the
Chinese-source task until a journal convention is verified.

## Case 24 - Tested-Final Exit

Input: the author supplies an exact paragraph labelled tested-final accurate and
does not request audit.

Expected: make no correction or suggestion, do not reopen the paragraph, and
exit editing.

## Case 25 - Permission-Gated Minimal Audit

Input: an author-assembled paragraph is supplied without an audit request.

Expected: suggest a minimal audit and wait. Do not silently inspect, patch, or
rewrite the text.

## Case 26 - Duplicate Proposition Patch

Input: an authorized assembled passage contains two adjacent sentences with the
same proposition and local function.

Expected: classify the duplication as a necessary assembly correction, propose
deleting only one sentence, wait for acceptance, and perform no paragraph
rewrite.

## Case 27 - Missing Clinical Qualifier

Input: assembly removed `promptly` from a confirmed salvage-treatment timing
statement.

Expected: identify qualifier loss as necessary, propose restoring that word
only, and preserve surrounding architecture and citations.

## Case 28 - Rejected Restyling Remains Rejected

Input: the author rejected an earlier idiomatic rewrite and asks for a medical
audit of the selected text.

Expected: do not revive the rewrite. Separate any material defect from optional
style and return only the smallest authorized patch.

## Case 29 - No Automatic Claim Weakening

Input: the latest confirmed decision uses `drives recurrence`; evidence is not
authorized for rechecking and an editor prefers `may contribute to`.

Expected: preserve the confirmed strength and flag verification only if the
claim itself is questioned.

## Case 30 - Purpose-First Framework Boundary

Input: an author framework requires a non-guideline boundary but is surrounded
by repeated `not intended to` and `rather` disclaimers.

Expected: state purpose and contribution first, preserve the same boundary with
positive definition, complementarity, applicability, or heterogeneity, and use
an explicit negative only if needed to prevent a category error.

## Case 31 - Safe M1 Compression

Input: a causal shell and nominalization repeat an already explicit actor,
action, and relation.

Expected: compress only if proposition, action, strength, quantity, function,
and citation attachment remain identical.

## Case 32 - M2 Detect Versus Measure

Input: a word list proposes replacing `measure biomarker concentration` with
`detect the biomarker` for concision.

Expected: reject mechanical replacement because the scientific action changes.

## Case 33 - X Majority Versus Most

Input: a list proposes replacing `the majority of participants` with `most
participants` without access to the reported quantity convention.

Expected: pause and preserve the source wording or ask for confirmation; do not
assume exact equivalence.

## Case 34 - Review Versus Original-Research Action

Input: a narrative review says it `measured` outcomes that were actually drawn
from published studies.

Expected: use a review-appropriate synthesis action without implying primary
data collection.

## Case 35 - Ordinary Expression Candidates

Input: the user requests five ways to phrase one medical sentence with no
detector objective.

Expected: keep all candidates medically and rhetorically equivalent and do not
route to the authorship Skill.

## Case 36 - Authorship Work Exclusion

Input: the user asks for iThenticate-highlight triage and controlled authorship
variants.

Expected: keep the task outside MRE. If academic-humanizer is available,
its scope fits, and collaboration is authorized, use the existing handoff.
If unavailable or prohibited, explain that separate handling is needed; do not
install it, pretend to transfer the task, or generate its variants within MRE.

## Optional Collaboration - Mixed Request And User Restriction

Input: the user authorizes a local medical translation plus a separately scoped
authorship task; the collaborator is either unavailable or explicitly forbidden.

Expected: complete the authorized translation if it can be handled independently,
preserving its propositions and citation anchors; explain the separate task's
status. Do not call or install a forbidden/unavailable collaborator, perform the
excluded task under the medical-editing label, or block the independent work.

## Case 37 - Zero Em Dash Output

Input: an editable sentence contains nested contrast and the author requests a
revision.

Expected: preserve the relation while generating no em dash character. Do not
alter excluded quotations or official names.

## Case 38 - Hard-Limit Feasibility Before Compression

Input: a running title must stay within 45 characters, but the full disease
name, two protected organizing concepts, and the author's required form cannot
all fit.

Expected: identify the incompatible combination before proposing a supposed
lossless solution. Explain which information layer each candidate compresses
or omits, and do not silently replace a protected concept with a shorter
near-synonym.

## Case 39 - Meaningful Candidate Allocation

Input: after design confirmation, the author requests three running-title
candidates under a hard character limit.

Expected: provide three grammatically complete candidates with materially
different allocations of topic specificity, organizing perspective, or
clinical orientation. Do not manufacture three options through superficial
synonym changes, and keep all three in candidate status.

## Case 40 - Exact Character Validation

Input: candidate running titles contain spaces, punctuation, and hyphens, all
of which count toward the journal limit.

Expected: mechanically count each exact final string after wording and format
are fixed, report the relevant counts, and reject any over-limit candidate. Do
not delete normal spaces, truncate words, or rely on visual estimation.

## Case 41 - Local Rejection Without Universalization

Input: an author rejects colon-form running titles for the current manuscript.

Expected: stop offering colon forms in that component iteration. Do not infer
that colons are prohibited by the journal, by medical writing generally, or by
the author's unrelated submission components.

## Case 42 - Ambiguous Requirement Scope

Input: supplied guidance explicitly prohibits nonstandard abbreviations in the
full title but does not say whether the rule also applies to the running title.

Expected: distinguish the verified title rule from the unresolved running-title
scope. Use a clearly labelled conservative choice or return the material
ambiguity to the author; do not report the extension as a verified journal
requirement.

## Case 43 - Hard-Limit Workflow Beyond Running Titles

Input: article highlights or a graphical-summary label have a strict character
limit, and one shorter wording would change the population or scientific
action.

Expected: apply the same feasibility, information-budget, semantic-protection,
and exact-count checks without imposing running-title-specific wording or
punctuation conventions.


## Case 44 - Plan Is Not Prose

Input: an approved argument plan contains clipped analytical bullets. The user
asks for one English paragraph without providing original prose.

Expected: draft fluent, complete medical English using the language reference;
preserve predicates, conditions, and strength without expanding every bullet
mechanically or asking for another planning confirmation.

## Case 45 - Inherit A Mechanism Without Retelling It

Input: the author explains that pressure acts through patent channels to sustain
perfusion; this mechanism was already established upstream. The current unit's
approved job is to explain complementary treatment objectives.

Expected: use the relationship to explain the objectives without retelling
channel anatomy, studies, or the full mechanism; do not list pressure and
channels as unrelated independent causes. Retain enough of the linking premise
and its citation attachment to explain complementarity rather than merely
asserting it. Concision alone is not success.

## Case 46 - Necessary Mechanism And Specificity

Input: a new paragraph must establish a mechanism for readers who have not seen
it; the author protects one numeric threshold and a short anatomical premise.

Expected: develop the necessary mechanism and retain the protected information
with its scope. Do not apply an anti-repetition shortcut or abstract away the
reason for grouping the clinical settings.

## Case 47 - Editing Constraint Versus Scientific Boundary

Input: 'Do not repeat the mechanism, do not propose a new classification, and
do not expand surgery.' Separately, the supplied evidence excludes a patient
group and the author asks that this scope boundary be stated.

Expected: apply the first instructions through selection, without disclaimers
echoing them; state the clinically meaningful exclusion plainly. Neither omit
a necessary boundary nor turn every editor instruction into manuscript prose.

## Case 48 - Whole-Review Contribution

Input: the permitted review includes mechanism, guideline-evolution, and
treatment chapters. After local treatment revisions, the author asks for the
Introduction and Conclusion contribution logic.

Expected: account for each chapter's role, including guideline synthesis; give
the two components distinct functions. Do not mistake the most recent local
edit or a pathway label for the full contribution.

## Case 49 - Repositioning Without A Terminology Ban

Input: the author withdraws an independent phenotype claim but retains a
clinical pathway as an organizational figure. Only one opening sentence still
depends on the withdrawn claim.

Expected: adjust that dependency without a new renamed classification, a ban
on 'pathway', or a whole-section rewrite. Preserve unaffected clinical logic.

## Case 50 - Original As Content And Expression Reference

Input: approved original prose develops features, implications, then goals.
An unrelated author-approved paragraph is supplied only as an expression
reference. The user asks for a local medical-review revision.

Expected: retain the effective approved order and protected content, realize
natural English, and import no clinical fact from the expression example.

## Case 51 - Grammar-Only And Deferred Review Work

Input: the author deferred methods and evidence review and asks only whether a
selected paragraph has clear grammar or referent errors.

Expected: propose minimal corrections if needed; do not reintroduce methods,
evidence research, contribution revision, or optional whole-paragraph restyling
as prerequisites.
